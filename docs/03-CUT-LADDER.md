# Cut ladder

Decided calmly, in advance. If something breaks close to a deadline and cannot be fixed in
time, features are switched off in exactly this order.

🚫 **It is not renegotiated at three in the morning.** That is precisely when bad scope
decisions get made. The list exists so nobody has to decide while tired.

| Order | What goes | What stays | Real cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | **F8 colour families** | The wardrobe and the traffic light stay whole | A good image for the video. Nothing structural |
| **2nd** | **F14 scarf/hat render** | The accessory **stays in the wardrobe and in every verdict**, it just is not rendered | Low: the text already carries the verdict, and for our user text is the real channel |
| **3rd** | **F9 duplicate alert** | — | Low, and so cheap it will probably never need to go |
| **4th** | **F16 corrector verification** | **F15 stays.** Recommending a corrector from a measurement is the core of the skin story | High. Declared as roadmap, **with the reason** |
| **5th** | **F17 MCP run** → documented only | Try-on keeps its REST path underneath | Medium |
| **6th** | **F5 live dual view** → a precomputed static comparison | The idea survives in the video | Low if precomputed well |
| **NEVER** | **F1 · F2 · F3 · F4 · F7 · F11 · F12 · F13** | | They are the project |

## Golden rule

When torn between **finishing one more feature** and **polishing what exists**: polish
wins, always.

Design is 25% of the score. A half-finished feature costs points on two criteria; one
feature fewer, well finished, costs none — **especially when the limit is declared**,
because declaring a limit is worth more than pretending it does not exist.
